# Methodology

This document describes *how* ApexLens turns timing data into estimates. Results and their numbers live
in `reports/` and are regenerated from the stored outputs; the same rules are served to the dashboard by
`GET /api/methodology` (`services/methodology.py`).

## 1. Lap cleaning

Every lap is kept. Each lap gets `is_clean` and, when not clean, the first matching `exclusion_reason`:
no lap time, non-positive time, unresolved driver, unknown track status, red flag, safety car, virtual
safety car, yellow flag, pit-in lap, pit-out lap, race start lap, deleted by race control, generated by
FastF1, timing not accurate, unknown compound. Weather condition per lap (`dry`, `damp`, `wet`, `unknown`)
combines the rain sensor with the tyre fitted.

* **Session reference** — median of all clean laps of all drivers in the session.
* **Relative pace (target)** — `rel_pace_pct = 100 × (lap / reference − 1)`; negative is faster. This
  removes circuit length, track evolution between sessions and most of the weather level.
* **Representative lap** — a clean lap no more than 5% slower than the driver's own median clean lap in
  that session. It removes traffic, mistakes and damage outliers without looking at other drivers.

Data-quality checks (`cleaning/validation.py`) run per partition with explicit thresholds. Fatal failures
(duplicate lap keys, non-positive times, too many unresolved identities, a driver in two cars, mixed
sessions) exclude the partition from modelling; warnings are reported and the data is kept.

## 2. Features and leakage

Each feature in `features/definitions.py` declares an *availability* time. Form features are
exponentially weighted means (half-life 4 events) over events that finished before the event's first
session, implemented as a shifted EWMA per driver/team; circuit history uses earlier seasons only. The
`pre_race` horizon adds the same weekend's qualifying result. Scenario inputs (tyre, weather, race
phase) are conditions the user sets or that were observed on the lap; they are not forecasts.

## 3. Model A — lap pace

* Population: representative race and sprint laps of the ingested seasons (2022–2025, one regulation era).
* Portfolio: historical group median (most specific of driver × compound × race phase, falling back to
  coarser groups with ≥ 20 laps), a team-adjusted form baseline,
  Ridge, a spline GAM (Ridge on B-splines of key continuous inputs) and LightGBM with Huber loss.
* Linear models exclude session-constant inputs from their main effects (the target is already centred
  per session), impute training medians and winsorise inputs at training percentiles to stop
  extrapolation on new circuits.
* Selection: lowest MAE on development walk-forward folds. The final season is a holdout.
* Intervals: split-conformal (90%) with separate calibration quantiles for dry and wet laps
  (Mondrian), calibrated on the most recent training events.
* Model uncertainty: 20 LightGBM refits on event-cluster bootstrap resamples; their spread is shown
  separately from the predictive interval.

## 4. Driver fingerprint

Two stages, so that car performance and race conditions are not credited to the driver:

1. **Per-session adjustment** (`driver_profiles/session_adjust.py`): OLS of representative-lap pace on
   driver indicators plus compound, compound-specific tyre age and lap number. Terms that are not
   identifiable in a session are dropped in a fixed order and the adjustment used is recorded.
   Standard errors are inflated for AR(1) correlation between consecutive laps.
2. **Crossed random effects** (`driver_profiles/mixed_model.py`): driver, driver-season and team-season
   effects with session fixed effects, fitted by REML via Henderson's mixed-model equations. The
   team-season effect is the car proxy; driver effects are identified by team-mate comparisons and by
   drivers who changed teams.

Condition-specific deviations (wet race, wet qualifying, street, high-speed and slow-corner circuits)
are a driver's residual from their own dry, car-adjusted expectation, shrunk with empirical Bayes.
Below the evidence thresholds a driver is reported as *insufficient evidence*; when the 90% posterior
interval includes zero, as *not distinguishable from zero*. Clusters (Model E: PCA + k-means with
bootstrap ARI) are exploratory and described only by the measured features that separate them.

## 5. Track geometry and corners

From each driver's fastest qualifying lap: the path is resampled every 2 m and smoothed
(Savitzky–Golay); curvature gives corner candidates. FastF1 corner markers are used when they lie on the
driven path and otherwise replaced by curvature-detected corners. Laps are aligned to the reference by a
guided projection (nearest point within ±150 m of the lap's own integrated distance), which is robust to
circuits that cross themselves. Corners are classified by minimum speed (slow < 125 km/h, fast ≥ 200
km/h; flat-out when the throttle stays ≥ 95% and the minimum speed is ≥ 97% of the entry speed) with hairpin, chicane, esses and sweeper flags — explicit
ApexLens conventions, not FIA classes. Team-mate corner deltas are only computed for comparable runs
(same qualifying segment or within 15 minutes, lap times within 1.5%).

## 6. Other models

* **B — sector pace**: relative sector time with and without driver corner-category profiles.
* **C — tyre degradation**: fuel-corrected stint slopes (stints ≥ 14 laps, the first five laps anchor the
  level); compound, circuit and driver-specific slopes compared predictively, walk-forward.
* **D — driver comparison**: the difference of two Model A predictions under one scenario, with an
  ensemble interval for the difference and counterfactuals (same car, conditions switched).
* **F — qualifying gap**: pre-event gap to pole (dry sessions), evaluated with MAE and per-event rank
  metrics.

## 7. Race week planner (Model G)

For a chosen race weekend, driver and (optionally) another team's car, the planner gives the weather
assumption, the best dry tyre strategy with pit windows, expected lap times, the predicted finishing
order and, for past races, what actually happened. Everything is estimated from data available before
the race start (`src/apexlens/strategy/`):

* **Strategy costs** — per lap, a compound pace offset (compared within driver-races at 8 laps of tyre
  age; extrapolating to age 0 was unstable) plus degradation × tyre age (Model C slopes, recency-weighted
  and shrunk to the circuit), plus the measured green-flag pit-stop loss per stop. Tyre life is capped at
  the 90th percentile of race stint length for that compound. FIA rules for single events (e.g. tyre-life
  caps in Qatar, two mandatory stops in Monaco 2025) are applied.
* **Optimisation** — an exact dynamic programme over pit laps for every compound sequence with 1–3 stops
  and at least two dry compounds. Pit windows are the laps at which a stop stays within a tolerance of
  that plan's optimum. Orderings of the same tyres cost the same in this model; ties go to the compound
  teams most often start this race on.
* **Calibration** — two hidden costs that lap times cannot show (track position lost per stop; limited
  new SOFT sets and graining per SOFT stint) are fitted on earlier races only, to agree with the plan most
  often used by the top-10 finishers.
* **Pace and finishing order** — the lap-pace model trained on earlier seasons gives each car's pace;
  the finishing order blends grid position and predicted pace with a weight fitted on earlier races.
* **Robustness** — parameters re-estimated on race-level bootstrap resamples; the share of resamples in
  which each plan is best is reported.
* **Weather** — user values, then the forecast (Open-Meteo, upcoming races), then the weekend's sessions,
  then the circuit's history. Temperature is shown but does not change tyre wear: within-circuit
  temperature adjustments made held-out degradation predictions worse.

The backtest (`reports/race_strategy.md`) compares every plan with the race; copying the previous race
at the same circuit is the main baseline.

## 8. Explanations

* Exact TreeSHAP for LightGBM and exact additive contributions for the linear models; grouped
  contributions per feature family.
* Global: mean |contribution|, grouped permutation importance on the holdout, accumulated local
  effects (ALE) which respect correlated inputs.
* Local: a narrative that lists the largest contributions in seconds and percent, the evidence behind
  the prediction (laps, events, circuit history) and warnings when inputs fall outside the training
  range.
* Validation (`explainability/validation.py`): additivity, agreement with the `shap` library, stability
  across bootstrap refits, sensitivity to small perturbations, agreement across horizons and with Ridge,
  and a list of correlated input pairs whose credit SHAP may split arbitrarily.

Explanations describe what the models learned from observational data. They are never presented as
causes: a "+0.2% from tyre age" contribution means the model associates older tyres with slower laps in
similar conditions, not that the tyre caused that exact loss on that lap.
